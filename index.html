Before you go in

Blue holes have hazards a beach does not: a sheer drop to 202 metres, unusual currents, cold layers and dark water. Read these at the rim.

Dean's Blue Hole safety

Ten rules for swimmers, snorkelers and divers

The water

  • This is an advanced environmentOnly strong swimmers and trained divers should go over the deep section.
  • The bottom disappearsThe floor drops away suddenly. Stay near the edges unless you are trained.
  • Watch for currentsAvoid siphon-like pulls at narrow openings in the rock.
  • Expect temperature shiftsThermoclines and dark zones arrive without warning as you go down.

Your dive

  • Never dive aloneKeep a buddy at the surface whenever you go deep.
  • Watch your buoyancyYou can sink faster than you expect.
  • Keep the exit in sightThe vertical walls can confuse which way is up and out.

At the rim

  • Snorkel above the rimStrong pulls can occur near the centre.
  • Avoid the centre if untrainedThe safest water is the outer, shallow basin.
  • Respect the warningsFollow every posted sign and rule at the site.

If someone goes under

Drowning is a lack of oxygen, not a heart problem first. That changes the order: breaths come before compressions.

First, get them out safely. Do not enter the water if it puts you in danger. Bring the person to a firm, safe surface.

Check for a response. Tap and shout "Are you OK?"

Call 911, or point at someone and tell them to call. Early help matters more than anything.

Then check breathing for no more than 10 seconds. Open the airway; look, listen and feel. Gasping is not normal breathing.

Give 2 rescue breaths first. Watch for the chest to rise. This is the step most people skip.

Then start compressions. Push hard and fast in the centre of the chest, 2 inches (5 cm) deep, 100 to 120 a minute. 30 compressions, 2 breaths, repeat.

How long

If you are alone and trained, give CPR for 2 minutes before leaving to get help. Keep going until they breathe, a professional takes over, or you are exhausted.

Use an AED

As soon as one arrives, switch it on and follow its voice prompts.

If they vomit

It is common after drowning. Turn them on their side, clear the mouth, and go straight back to CPR.

Freedive, snorkel, scuba

Each way of going under has its own checklist. Run through yours before every entry.

Freediving

Before

  • Never freedive alone.
  • Rest and breathe normally. Do not hyperventilate.
  • Check conditions.
  • Warm up gently.

During

  • Equalise early and often.
  • Move slowly to save energy.
  • Know your limit and turn early.
  • Never ascend too fast.

At the surface

  • Take recovery breaths.
  • Signal "OK" to your buddy.

Snorkeling

Where

  • Stay in sight of the shore or boat.
  • Choose calm water.
  • Wear bright colours.

How

  • Breathe slowly and relax.
  • Use fins that fit.
  • Do not kick sand or coral.

Stay aware

  • Look up for boats often.
  • Do not touch marine life.
  • If tired, float on your back.

Recreational scuba

Before

  • Check your gear carefully.
  • Review hand signals with your buddy.
  • Plan depth, time and route.

During

  • Equalise often.
  • Hold your buoyancy.
  • Watch your air and your buddy.
  • Move calmly to save air.

After

  • Drink water.
  • Follow the no-fly rules.

An Ice Age sinkhole

Blue holes formed when caverns in the limestone collapsed and were flooded as sea levels rose after the last Ice Age. Their dark navy colour, visible even from the air, comes from that depth.

Its entrance, a modest 30 metres across, opens into a cavern nearly 100 metres wide, closed on three sides by a natural rock amphitheatre and on the fourth by a turquoise lagoon.

Freediving and records

The hole is the yearly home of Vertical Blue, one of the most respected competitions in freediving. Athletes come from around the world to reach extraordinary depths on a single breath. William Trubridge swam to 92 metres here without fins in 2010. The sport has its cost too: Nicholas Mevoli died after a dive here in 2013.

Today

Dean's Blue Hole is still a place of wonder: natural beauty, geological mystery and human ambition in one circle of water. It invites people to be bold and curious, and to respect the techniques and safety practices that deep water demands.

Among the many blue holes found around the world, few compare with the beauty and significance of this one.
